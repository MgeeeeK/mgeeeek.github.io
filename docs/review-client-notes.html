Client Walkthrough — Not for Publication 15 viewports · 8 routes · Chromium via Playwright

I sat down to approve my own portfolio. Here's what stopped me.

I went through every page of the site the way a stranger would — the way a recruiter half-interested in hiring me would. I clicked everything that looked clickable, filled in the form, then went back and did it again on my MacBook Air, my old MacBook Pro, a 1440p monitor, a borrowed 4K display, and an iPad in both orientations. This is the unfiltered list of what I found, worst first.

Gut reaction

The design is genuinely charming and nothing crashes — but the wider device sweep made things worse, not better. My resume link still 404s, an entire piece of diary work I apparently designed doesn't exist anywhere on the page, and if anyone opens this on an iPad my case studies are missing words. I can't send this to anyone until the critical list is clear.

Critical

Case-study text becomes unreadable on every iPad size between 768px and 1279px

I checked every case study at iPad Air, iPad Pro 12.9", and iPad mini widths, portrait and landscape. Below the site's canonical 1280px width, the "canvas" container shrinks to fit the screen, but the text and images inside it are still pinned to the exact pixel coordinates built for 1280px — so they run straight off the visible edge instead of reflowing. On Email Strategy, the "Tone of Voice" section disappears entirely at 820px. On Social Media Brand Identity, Internal Comms, and Fully Filmy, whole lines of body copy and image cards are sliced off at the right edge — in places badly enough to lose entire words. It gets worse as the screen narrows and clears up completely at 1280px and above.

Why it matters: this is every single iPad — far and away the most common device I'd expect a recruiter to skim a portfolio on after a phone and a laptop.

confirmed at 820×1180, 1024×1366, 1133×744, 1180×820 across social-media-brand-identity, internal-comms, fully-filmy, email-strategy — resolves at ≥1280px

Major

The homepage doesn't center itself the way every case study does, and it shows on a real monitor

Every case-study page uses a container capped at `width: min(100%, 1280px)` with auto margins, so it centers gracefully on anything wider — I confirmed this holds up cleanly all the way to 4K. The homepage's Work and Contact sections never got that same treatment; they're left-aligned at a fixed width with no auto-centering. On a normal 1080p monitor this leaves 700–1000px of dead space on the right. On a 4K display, the entire Work grid and contact form sit pooled in the left third of the screen. It's the one part of the site that looks unfinished on hardware most people actually own.

components/WorkSection/WorkSection.module.css and components/ContactSection/ContactSection.module.css — width set, no margin:auto (compare to any app/work/*/page.module.css .canvas rule)

To be fair — what actually works
  • The Instagram lightbox on Social Media Brand Identity is genuinely impressive: it pulls real post data, real like counts, real thumbnails, and opens inline without ever leaving the page. That's the bar the rest of the site should be held to.
  • Nothing crashes. Across all eight routes I found zero console errors — only preload warnings. The code underneath this is stable.
  • The visual identity — folders, washi-tape marquees, the hand-drawn underlines, the stamped badge — is distinctive and holds together. It doesn't feel templated.
  • The case-study writing itself is sharp and specific throughout; that's Abhi's voice coming through, not a design problem to fix.
  • The centering strategy the case-study pages use (a 1280px canvas with auto margins) is exactly right, and it holds up cleanly all the way from a 14" laptop to a 4K display — the homepage just needs to catch up to a pattern the rest of the site already got correct.
  • Nav bar and its dropdown never broke, at any of the fifteen viewport sizes I tried, including the tightest iPad landscape heights. That's a lot of surface area to get right and it did.

None of this is "start over" territory — the bones and the personality are both good. But the wider device pass changed my priorities, not just my count: a broken resume link, an entire missing diary case study, and text that goes unreadable on every iPad size are three separate reasons a real visitor bounces before they ever get to judge the writing. Fix the critical eight, bring the homepage's centering in line with the rest of the site, and give Fun Stuff a way to be found on a phone — then we talk about the rest.
